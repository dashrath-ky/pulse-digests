Finshots · Mon Aug 31
Onion prices have crossed ₹60 a kg across much of India, and the government is running a dedicated freight train of buffer stock to the worst-hit cities. The odd part is that India actually grows more onions than it eats. The problem is timing and storage: one harvest, the March-to-May rabi crop, is the only one that keeps well, so it has to carry the whole country from June to November, and a big chunk of it rots in open sheds before it ever gets there. Unseasonal rain in April made this year worse. Farmers have no minimum support price and no futures market to hedge with, so they eat the swings while the retail price runs far ahead of what they were paid. A CCI study once found onions bought at ₹34 a kg selling for ₹80.
Lenny's Newsletter · Sun Aug 30
Tara Seshan, who runs product for Codex and ChatGPT Work at OpenAI, argues we're entering a phase where AI stops being a tool you prompt and starts being a coworker that sticks around. Her framing: people move from rowing to steering, so judgment and ambition become the things that separate good work from average. She also says OpenAI builds for what the models will be able to do two or three months out, not what they can do today.
