ByteByteGo · Tue Sep 01
A good walkthrough of why you can't just run a 70-billion-parameter model on a normal GPU, and what people do about it. The model is 140 GB of weights while a very good graphics card has 48 GB, and models have grown roughly 100x while consumer GPU memory has only doubled. The fix is shrinking the model three ways: store each weight in fewer bits, cut the pathways that barely fire, or train a smaller model to imitate the big one.
Lenny's Newsletter · Tue Sep 01
Anshu Chimala, who led design R&D at Apple for 12 years, argues AI isn't bad at design, we just prompt it badly. Models are trained to pick the most likely next token, which is the opposite of good design, so you get safe, bland output by default. His process is to push the model past its comfortable patterns first, chain different models together, then polish the rough edges.
