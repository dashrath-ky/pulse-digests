Kubernetes & GPU Workloads
NVIDIA open-sourced Snapshot, a tool that saves a fully started GPU inference server to disk and brings it back later instead of booting from scratch. On B200s it restored vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM replicas in 4 to 43 seconds, which NVIDIA says is 8x to 36x faster than a cold start, and even a 1 TB model spread over eight GPUs came back in under 43 seconds. The catch is that a restore needs the same GPU model and driver as the checkpoint. This goes straight at slow replica startup during autoscaling, which is a big part of what makes spot and scale-from-zero painful.

Kubernetes & GPU Workloads
NVIDIA also released two open-source projects for running your own managed Kubernetes on GPU machines. NVKR builds repeatable node and container images, and NVKE creates and manages clusters through a REST API. Both are public previews that NVIDIA says aren't ready for production, but with Firmus, SK Telecom and platform vendors like Rafay and Spectro Cloud signing on, this looks like NVIDIA's play to own the cluster layer that neoclouds run on.

Kubernetes & GPU Workloads
Agent Substrate, NVIDIA's runtime for running many sandboxed AI agents, was donated to the CNCF Sandbox. It keeps a small pool of warm pods and swaps agent sessions in and out of them, saving an idle session's memory and files and restoring it on any free worker. NVIDIA claims about 10x more agents per machine than plain containers, and it plugs into the Dynamo inference stack and the KAI scheduler. The APIs are still changing, and its GPU support is being rebuilt on Kubernetes' new device allocation API.
