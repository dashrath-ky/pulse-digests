Finshots · Sun Sep 06
Researchers are using data to figure out which foods taste good together, and the answers are strange. A team at IIIT-Delhi built FlavorDB, a database of 936 ingredients and roughly 26,000 flavour molecules. It turns out coffee and capsicum share 78 molecules, and coffee even shares some with crab. The practical angle is cost: one food entrepreneur used it to hit a raisin-like flavour in a ragi biscuit using cardamom oil instead of actual raisins, skipping rounds of expensive trial and error.
Finshots · Sun Sep 06
American sunscreen is genuinely worse than what you can buy in Europe or Asia, and the reason is regulatory. The US treats sunscreen as an over-the-counter drug, so every UV-blocking ingredient needs FDA approval, and the last new one was cleared in 1999. The US has 16 approved filters versus 29 in Europe and 30-plus in Japan and South Korea. On average, US sunscreens deliver only about 24% of the UVA protection their SPF number implies, in a country where skin cancer is the most common cancer and costs the health system around $8.9 billion a year.

A clean explainer on how MCP, RAG, and AI agents differ, since they get lumped together constantly. MCP is a standard protocol for wiring models to tools and data so you don't hand-build every integration. RAG fetches fresh documents at query time so the model doesn't invent answers. An agent is the thing that actually decides and acts on its own, rather than just replying.

VMs versus containers, explained by what actually differs. A VM boots a whole operating system with its own kernel, so it's heavy but takes minutes to start and can run Windows and Linux side by side. Containers share the host kernel, so they start in milliseconds, but every container has to be compatible with that one kernel.
