Finshots · Sun Sep 27
Buddhist temples in Japan are quietly becoming investors. Fewer people follow Buddhism than 20 years ago, funerals (their main income) have gotten smaller since COVID, and costs keep climbing with a weak yen and rising wages. One Hokkaido priest, a former stockbroker, paid for $162,000 of temple repairs by putting the temple's savings into US Treasuries, Japanese property funds and stocks like BYD, earning over 10% a year. It's a sign of how much Japan has changed after decades of near-zero inflation and idle cash.
Finshots · Sun Sep 27
An Australian startup called In Truth wants to turn a whole community's stress into a single number. Last month, 131 groups wore fitness trackers like Garmin, Whoop and Oura, and their heart data plus journal entries were rolled into a 0 to 100 "Peace Score." The pitch is that governments budget around numbers, so a stress score could unlock mental health funding. Finshots is skeptical: Bhutan has tracked happiness for 50 years, and the WHO says countries still spend only about 2% of health budgets on mental health.
Morning Brew · Sat Sep 26
US mortgage rates went above 7% this week for the first time since early 2025. It follows the Fed's first rate hike in three years, driven by inflation from the Iran war. Rates had dipped below 6% in February, but the spring selling season flopped, and one Moody's economist says 8% isn't off the table. Home sales are already slipping, and more buyers are turning to riskier adjustable-rate loans.

A useful explainer on MCP versus plain function calling. Both let an AI model ask to use a tool, and the app around it runs the tool and hands back the result. The difference is where the tool lives: function calls run on your own machine, while MCP lets the agent call tools hosted on remote servers, which opens it up to a huge public catalog.
