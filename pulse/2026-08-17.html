Finshots · Mon Aug 17
Finshots ran its post-Independence Day special on how the Indian rupee actually became the rupee. Before 1935 there was no RBI, and Indian notes were printed in Britain until a press opened in Nashik in 1928. The odd bit is what came after 1947: the rupee was still split into 16 annas until the 1957 Coinage Act moved it to 100 paise, and shops quietly rounded 25-paise items up to 26, which was enough to start fights at the counter.
Finshots · Mon Aug 17
The messiest chapter was splitting the money at Partition. The RBI had to keep printing currency for Pakistan for a while, and of the roughly ₹75 crore Pakistan was owed, ₹55 crore got held back as the Kashmir dispute heated up. Pakistan cut the RBI loose early, in April 1948, and India only released the money after Gandhi went on a fast unto death over it.
Morning Brew · Sun Aug 16
Raw milk is having a real moment in the US despite doctors telling people not to drink it. Farmers report growing sales at $20+ a gallon, dozens of states are trying to loosen the rules, and a bipartisan House bill would let it cross state lines. The CDC counted 2,600 people sickened and 225 hospitalisations from raw milk between 1998 and 2019.
Morning Brew · Sun Aug 16
The dairy industry is still fighting over who gets to say "milk". The UK Supreme Court told Oatly it can't use the word, a Swiss court struck down Alpro's "Shhh…This Is Not M*lk", and the FDA is fine with it as long as the label is clear. Meanwhile plant-based milk sales by volume have fallen more than 5% three years running.

Milk is turning up in skincare too, in creams, soaps and serums, sometimes from goats, buffalo, or donkeys. Cleopatra's milk baths get the credit; Hailey Bieber's Rhode is the modern version.
Lenny's Newsletter · Sun Aug 16
Lenny interviewed Ian Silber, OpenAI's head of product design, who's run design for ChatGPT and Codex for three years. His argument is that this is the best time in history to be a designer, but he's also blunt that AI has made engineers roughly 10x more productive while design teams haven't seen anything close to that. His advice to his own designers is just to do less.
