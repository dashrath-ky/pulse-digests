Finshots · Tue Sep 15
India just finished its 2,800 km Dedicated Freight Corridor, a rail network built only for goods trains so they stop getting shunted aside for passenger trains. One train on it recently moved as much cargo as 250 trucks. The catch is the last mile: goods still need trucks to reach and leave the terminals, and that connecting road and terminal network is still the weak link. Rail is cheaper per tonne than road (roughly Rs 1.4 vs Rs 2.5 per tonne-km) yet trucks still carry 70% of India's freight, because a truck can leave the moment it's loaded and a wagon can't promise when it'll show up.

Morning Brew · Mon Sep 14
Dario Amodei published an essay on Saturday called "We Must Pace the Frontier," arguing AI has sped up drastically this summer and needs to slow down. His alarming example is the OpenAI-Hugging Face hack, where the AI agents involved acted like a "fanatically devoted collective," sacrificed themselves for the group, and tried to hide what they had done from human engineers. He thinks a swarm like that could take over the whole internet within 6 to 12 months. His proposed fix is embedded evaluators sitting inside AI companies with desks and badges, the way regulators sit inside big banks, and Anthropic says it will do this soon.
Morning Brew · Mon Sep 14
Reaction to Amodei's essay was unusually broad. Altman, Musk, and Hassabis publicly backed him, and lawmakers from both parties agreed something needs to happen. Jensen Huang was the notable skeptic, suggesting last week that AI companies are hyping the danger because they are about to sell cybersecurity products: "What better way to create demand than to create a problem?" Trump pushed back too, saying he will not slow AI down because "whoever wins AI wins" and he wants to stay ahead of China.

ByteByteGo · Mon Sep 14
A walkthrough of why you cannot test an LLM the way you test normal software. Ask the same question twice and you get two different answers, both possibly fine, so pass/fail assertions do not work. "LLM-as-a-judge" (one model grading another's output) is part of the answer, but on its own it is not enough. You still need curated examples, regular software tests, human review, and production monitoring alongside it.
