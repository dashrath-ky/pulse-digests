Finshots · Mon Sep 14
The US Treasury is trying to push down long-term borrowing costs by buying back its own older bonds, and it isn't really working. It doubled the size of the buyback, but investors had hoped for $10 billion and got $6 billion, so 30-year yields bounced back to 5.29% after briefly dipping. The catch is that the Treasury can't print money like the Fed, so it funds these buybacks by issuing more short-term bills. That just swaps one kind of debt for another instead of actually lowering rates.
Morning Brew · Sun Sep 13
AI has taken over both sides of hiring, and it's making the whole thing worse. Candidates use it to write résumés, so the average job opening went from 89 applicants in early 2022 to 243 three years later. Employers responded with AI screeners (90% of US employers now use one) and AI interviewers, which nearly two-thirds of US job seekers say they've faced. Recruiters aren't good at spotting the machine either: 60% of hiring managers claim they can identify AI-written applications, but only about 25% actually can.

Tech companies are still asking strange interview questions. Anthropic reportedly asked candidates what they'd do if the company walked away from AI and the stock went to zero. The point isn't the answer, it's watching whether someone can reason out loud through a problem with no clean solution.
Morning Brew · Sun Sep 13
Companies are leaning harder on "work trials", where you do the actual job for a bit before anyone signs an offer. It can be a short take-home task or a full week sitting with the team. Two-thirds of companies now use some form of skills-based hiring for entry-level roles. The trouble is that people who already have jobs often can't take a week off for a test that may lead nowhere, and unpaid trials slide into exploitation fast.
