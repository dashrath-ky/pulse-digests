ByteByteGo · Mon Aug 31
A really good explainer on what happens in the second or two between hitting Enter in a chatbot and the first word appearing. The model never sees your message as you typed it, it gets a document assembled around it with a system prompt, tools, memory, and the entire conversation replayed from scratch, because these models remember nothing between turns. That's also why long chats get slower and pricier: turn one might process 1,100 tokens, turn twenty closer to 4,900.
Lenny's Newsletter · Mon Aug 31
A PM at Melio says Claude now handles 70 to 80% of his workday, and he explains how he got there. He spent months feeding it voice memos, decks, and brain dumps to build context files, then set up a weekly loop that compares Claude's drafts against what he actually sent and learns from the difference. Best bit: his morning brief flags terms it doesn't recognise and asks him to explain them. He's blunt that the first few weeks feel painfully slow before it pays off.
