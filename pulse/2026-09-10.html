Finshots · Thu Sep 10
Copper has crossed $14,500 a tonne in London, up roughly 16-17% this year and nearly 48% over twelve months. Supply is the problem: Chile's mines are hitting seismic risks, ageing infrastructure and poorer ore, and China stopped exporting the sulphuric acid needed to extract copper from low-grade rock so it could make fertiliser instead. Meanwhile EVs, grid upgrades and AI data centres all want far more copper than before, so the world is now heading into a shortage this year rather than the surplus everyone expected.

TLDR IT · Wed Sep 09
A nice explainer on shuffle-sharding, a cheap trick for stopping one noisy customer from taking down everyone else in a shared system. Instead of parking each tenant on a fixed shard, you give each one a random overlapping subset of workers, so almost no two tenants share exactly the same set. Costs no extra hardware, but you have to be OK with partial degradation.

ByteByteGo · Wed Sep 09
A walkthrough of smart model routing, which can cut LLM bills by around 10x. The idea is simple: stop sending every request to your most expensive model. Put a router in front that sizes up each request and sends the easy ones (classify this ticket) to a small cheap model while the hard ones go to the big one. The piece covers the main approaches, including cascading (try the cheap model first) and semantic routing, plus the ways routing tends to go wrong.
