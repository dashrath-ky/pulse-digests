Finshots · Sun Aug 30
Canada is threatening tariffs of up to 50% on American paper products from September, hitting back at US tariffs. Most toilet paper sold in America is made in America, but the pulp behind it largely comes from Canadian forests. So the price of one of the most boring things in the house could go up, which is a neat reminder that a tariff doesn't have to touch the finished product for shoppers to feel it.
Finshots · Sun Aug 30
There's a growing market for paid companionship. A "rent me for a day" rate card went viral in India last month (₹1,500 for a coffee date, ₹10,000 for a weekend getaway), and in Russia men are running real ads offering to show up at your barbecue for $13 to $65. Behavioural economist Dan Ariely's explanation is that paying a stranger keeps things clean: no goodwill owed, no awkward obligation on either side. AI companionship is riding the same wave, with apps like Replika putting voice calls and better memory behind a subscription.
Morning Brew · Sat Aug 29
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who normally gives markets nothing, finally said something at Jackson Hole: the Fed will have "work to do" if inflation doesn't cool. Traders read that as a rate hike coming, and the odds of one at next month's meeting jumped from 35% to 58% while he was still speaking. The 2-year Treasury yield climbed from 4.24% to 4.36% in the hours after.
