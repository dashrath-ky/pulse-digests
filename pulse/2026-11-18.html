GPUs & Capacity
Nvidia beat its own forecast in its first earnings report without Jensen Huang, with Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year ago, and guided to about $130 billion next quarter. Interim CEO Colette Kress also said Nvidia will stop selling H200s in China. Jefferies thinks the new rule barring US chips from foreign data centers that serve Chinese customers could cut $44 to $62 billion from Nvidia's data center revenue next year. Kress says the Vera Rubin backlog absorbs that, and that Nvidia has secured memory and packaging capacity and is working with TSMC to close the gap between supply and demand. For GPU buyers, demand still runs ahead of supply, so don't expect high-end capacity to get easier or cheaper soon. (also in The Verge, Morning Brew)

GPUs & Capacity
Anthropic told IPO investors that Claude Mythos did the software work to run one of its models on AMD MI355X GPUs in about three days, with almost no human help. That work, mostly writing the low-level GPU code called kernels, used to take six to 12 months. The claim only covers prompt processing at "a useful level of performance," and the old timeline is an estimate. Still, if porting to non-Nvidia chips really gets this cheap, mixed-accelerator fleets become far more practical to schedule and sell.

Inference Serving
NVIDIA explains how it co-designed sparse attention with its hardware for long-context inference. Agents and big codebases push prompts into the hundreds of thousands of tokens, and at that length the model can't afford to look at every token, so it attends to a chosen subset.
