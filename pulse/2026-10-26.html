Accelerators & Capacity
SemiAnalysis took a close look at Amazon's Trainium chips and says they are no longer just for inference. AWS's new PyTorch backend lets you run normal PyTorch code on Trainium, mostly by changing `.to("cuda")` to `.to("neuron")`. The authors trained Qwen3-32B on 16 Trainium2 chips at about 30% hardware use once compiled, though the first training step took several minutes to compile. Anthropic already runs production inference and RL on Trainium2 and 3, and AWS is adding SGLang support. If Trainium becomes a real PyTorch target, it becomes a real option for scheduling training and serving on AWS capacity, not only on NVIDIA GPUs.

Models & Research
Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro, a huge 2.8 trillion parameter model that only uses about 104 billion parameters for each token. On Artificial Analysis's overall score it lands 4th, just behind Opus 5.5 and ahead of Fable 5.1. That's why people say open models are now only about a month behind the best closed ones. The weights aren't out yet, but Moonshot promises them on November 4. The API costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, and a "Highspeed" tier at double the price claims 2 to 3 times faster output. Even squeezed down to 4-bit, the weights need about 1.4 TB of memory, so serving it takes a serious multi-GPU setup. (also in Interconnects)

Industry & Business
"Black October": in about 48 hours, a pile of serious security flaws and breaches went public, many of them found with AI models. They include an unauthenticated remote code execution bug in PHP, a top-severity GitLab bug going back to 2014, and LiteLLM 1.107.0 shipping with a password stealer. Railway also told customers to rotate their secrets, and a weak certificate authority key reportedly got cracked with one Claude prompt. Microsoft says a China-aligned group used an open-source AI hacking agent against more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants. Monthly vulnerability reports have reportedly jumped from about 4,000 to 15,000 since April. Some argue most of these bugs were found earlier and only disclosed now. Either way, if you use LiteLLM, check your version, and update pip, uv, and certifi to get the new CA list. (also in Interconnects)

Industry & Business
OpenAI and Anthropic have cut their prices again and again over the last few months. GPT-5.6 Luna dropped 80%, the GPT-6 family is about 75% cheaper overall, and Opus 5.5 costs roughly half of what Opus 5 did. Max Woolf's best guess at why is that cheap open-weight models are easy to swap in, with dozens of hosts serving DeepSeek. He doesn't think prices will climb back up.
