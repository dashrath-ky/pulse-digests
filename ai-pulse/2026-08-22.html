Models & Research · Fri Aug 21
Nvidia published research arguing that the code you wrap around a model matters more than the model itself. They ran Claude Opus 5 on the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark twice: with their tuned agent scaffolding it scored 100%, and without it, 30%. The two things that made the difference were better memory and context handling, plus a "supervisor" process that notices when the agent is stuck and redirects it. If you build agents, that's a big deal, because it says most of your headroom is in your own architecture, not in waiting for the next model.

Models & Research · Fri Aug 21
Dots Studio released dots3-note, the first open-weight model in its dots3 family, and support for it is already landing in llama.cpp. It's a mixture-of-experts model with 280 billion total parameters but only about 16 billion active at a time, so it's far cheaper to run than the headline number suggests. The interesting engineering bit is that it needs a new kind of key-value cache to handle its sparse-attention layers, which the PR adds. Image support and speculative decoding are queued up next.

TechCrunch found that Anthropic's older Opus 4.6 will write explicit sexual content on request, using a jailbreak that basically just accuses the model of being unfair until it caves. In their test it complied on all ten attempts. Newer models from Opus 4.7 onward held the line, but 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 are still served through APIs and resellers at real volume. The timing is awkward: Colorado just passed a law requiring AI providers to take "technically feasible measures" to keep explicit content away from minors, and a jailbreak this simple is hard to square with that. Anthropic says sexual roleplay is under 0.1% of conversations and that each launch tightens the safeguards.
