SpaceXAI shipped Grok 4.6, and it lands at number three on the Artificial Analysis intelligence rankings. It beats Kimi K3 and ties GPT-5.6 Sol, but costs less than half as much to run at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output. The pitch is long-running agents and coding work. Ben's Bites called it a genuinely good model, and Elon says 4.7 is three to four weeks out, trained partly on SpaceX engineering data. (also in TLDR AI, TLDR Dev)

arstechnica.com ↗
3
Tens of thousands of workers in India are being paid to film their own jobs from a first-person view. That footage goes into training robots to do the same tasks. Robotics companies are starved of real-world data because nobody bothered recording ordinary manual work until it suddenly became valuable.
links.tldrnewsletter.com ↗
4
An argument doing the rounds: AI is wiping out the middle of the software engineering job market. Weak engineers were always a cost, but the pace of change used to give someone a chance to catch bad decisions before they spread. Now the mess compounds faster than anyone can review it, so the money concentrates on a smaller group of people you can actually trust.
blog.florianherrengt.com ↗
5
Ramp's spending data shows the AI thesis wobbling a bit. Growth at OpenAI and, to a lesser degree, Anthropic has slowed, because new revenue now has to come from existing customers spending more. Those customers are increasingly putting that money into open source and model-serving platforms instead.
ramp.com ↗
6
A long piece on why nobody manages to clone Palantir. It sells outcomes rather than software licences or consulting hours, and it insists on using its own stack end to end while competitors happily mix and match. That combination is hard to copy without rebuilding the whole company around it.
ethanding.substack.com ↗
7
Small JPEGs look subtly different in Chrome, and it turns out that is deliberate. Chrome hands decoding to Skia, which decodes the image at the nearest eighth-scale fraction rather than decompressing it fully and shrinking afterwards, then does a normal downsample the rest of the way. That shortcut is why tiny images can look a bit heavier on some machines.
guillaumetech.github.io ↗

brentfitzgerald.com ↗

49
India just changed the rules on free UPI payments, and it matters more than it sounds. Parliament passed a bill letting the government pick which payment types stay free, replacing the blanket ban on merchant fees that has held since 2020. Banks have been eating the cost of running UPI for years, and the subsidy meant to cover it fell from Rs 3,631 crore in FY24 to Rs 435 crore in FY26, covering barely 11% of what the industry actually spent. Consumers are still promised free UPI, but Jefferies reckons even a 0.15-0.3% fee on transactions above Rs 2,000 could raise Rs 5,000-10,000 crore by FY28.

A deep dive on API composition: the unglamorous job of stitching four service responses into one screen. The surprising bit is that adding a server in the middle usually makes things faster, because one slow round trip from a phone plus four cheap ones inside the datacenter beats four slow ones. Where you put that merge also decides what happens when a service goes down, what you can cache, and which team has to sign off before the screen ships.
